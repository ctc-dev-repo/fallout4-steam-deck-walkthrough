# Fallout 4 on Steam Deck — Absolute Beginner's Walkthrough

Optimized for one thing: **you, lying on the couch with a Steam Deck, having a
good time without reading a wiki.** Follow the phases in order; each one tells
you what to do, what to ignore, and why.

Spoiler policy: Parts 1–7 are spoiler-light (objectives, not story reveals).
Part 8 contains **major endgame spoilers** behind a clear warning.

---

## Part 1 — Steam Deck setup (10 minutes, do this once)

Fallout 4 is Steam Deck **Verified** and runs via Proton with zero fiddling.
These settings make it better.

### In-game graphics (Settings → Display)

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Preset | Medium | Best quality/perf balance on Deck |
| Motion Blur | Off | Instant clarity win |
| Depth of Field | Off | See enemies, not blur |
| God Rays | Off | Big FPS hog, low payoff |
| Lens Flare | Off | Preference |
| Weapon Debris | **Off** | Known crash source |
| Ambient Occlusion | Off | Free FPS |
| Shadow Quality | Medium | Don't go lower — flashlights look broken |
| Anisotropic Filtering | 8 | Cheap, makes floors readable |
| Wetness / Snow | Off | Free FPS |
| Anti-Aliasing | TAA | Fine at 800p |

### Steam overlay settings (the UX magic)

1. **Frame cap:** Steam menu (⋯) → performance → set refresh to **40 Hz / 40 FPS
   cap, TDP 8 W**. Smooth, cool, quiet, ~3 h battery. (30 Hz / 30 FPS / 6 W if
   you want ~4.5 h on a flight.)
2. **Gyro aiming:** Controller settings → Gyro → "As Mouse" active *when touching
   right pad or aiming*. Fallout has no aim-assist; gyro is your aim-assist.
3. **Back grip buttons:** bind **L5 = F5 (Quicksave)** and **R5 = Q
   (Favorites)**. Steam Input can send these keyboard keys on top of the
   gamepad. Quicksaving from a grip button without opening a menu is the single
   biggest UX upgrade in this guide.
4. **Right trackpad:** set to Mouse (for terminals and menu precision).
5. **Subtitles:** Settings → Gameplay → Subtitle size **Large**. FO4 text is
   small at 800p; large subtitles fix the worst of it.
6. **Cloud saves** are on by default — your progress survives anything.

## Part 2 — Before you leave Vault 111

### Difficulty

Choose **Normal**. If a fight ever feels unfair, drop to Easy — Fallout 4 has
zero shame mechanics for lowering difficulty (XP is identical on all
settings). Do **not** pick Survival; it's a hardcore mode with no fast travel
and no normal saves. Not for beginners.

### S.P.E.C.I.A.L. (your stats)

You get 21 points to spread over 7 stats (each starts at 1). Every stat gates
perks you'll want eventually, so there are no fatal mistakes — but this spread
makes the early game forgiving:

| Stat | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4 | Carry weight + melee damage |
| Perception | 4 | VATS accuracy + lockpicking perk |
| Endurance | 5 | More HP = fewer reloads |
| Charisma | 3 | Better prices and companion persuasion |
| Intelligence | 5 | **+3% XP per point — levels you faster** |
| Agility | 5 | More AP for VATS + stealth |
| Luck | 2 | Crit meter fills faster (fine to shortchange early) |

Spend your first levels topping whichever stat matches how you're playing.

### First perks (levels 2–5, in order)

1. **Locksmith 1** — locks are everywhere
2. **Scrounger 1** — noticeably more ammo in containers
3. **Life Giver 1** — +20 HP
4. **Sneak 1** — being unseen is the beginner's best armor
5. **Rifleman 1** — if you like rifles (take **Commando 1** instead for
   automatic weapons)

### Habits that make the whole game better

- **Quicksave constantly** (L5 grip button). Before doors. After fights.
  Randomly. FO4 bugs are rare but saves are free.
- **Everything is loot, but weight is law.** You can only carry ~150 lbs.
  Grab: ammo, caps, stimpaks, duct tape, adhesive, screws, circuitry, oil.
  Ignore: most weapons (heavy!), pre-war junk you can't scrap.
- **"Tag for search"** in the junk tab (press X on an item) — items containing
  components you need get a magnifier icon everywhere. Game-changing.

## Part 3 — Prologue & Sanctuary (Levels 1–2)

1. **Character creation:** name, appearance (or randomize), then SPECIAL per
   Part 2. Read the book on the floor ("You're S.P.E.C.I.A.L.") for one free
   stat point.
2. Follow the story beats. The prologue is unskippable (~20 min first time).
3. **210 years later:** leave the cryo pod, grab the **10mm pistol and
   Stimpaks from the Overseer's desk** (the vault is otherwise empty of loot —
   don't waste 20 minutes searching).
4. Exit the vault. Follow the marker **home**. Loot your old house (baby room
   has a You're S.P.E.C.I.A.L. book if you missed it).
5. Meet **Codsworth** (your robot butler) in Sanctuary. He fights for you —
   let him. The raiders nearby drop pipe pistols and a bit of ammo.
6. **Loot Sanctuary houses** for junk (duct tape, adhesive, TVs) but don't
   overdo it — you'll be back.

## Part 4 — Concord & your first Power Armor (Level 1–3)

Follow the river south to Concord (marker: **First Steps**).

1. **Museum of Freedom:** fight up the stairs with Codsworth + Preston Garvey
   on the balcony. Use cover; raiders die fast to the 10mm.
2. **Power Armor time:** Preston sends you to the basement → fuse the
   generator → take the **T-45 Power Armor** and the **laser musket**.
   - Power Armor = damage reduction + super strength, but it runs on **fusion
     cores**. This one is nearly full. Cores drain only while *sprinting* in
     armor — walk normally to save charge.
3. **The Deathclaw** (your first boss):
   - Get in Power Armor, use the laser musket (hold to charge, 2–3 crank
     minimum)
   - **Use VATS** (L4/L5 shoulder button... it's on the left bumper) to tag it
     — VATS accuracy through dust is how you win
   - Keep moving; never let it corner you in the museum lobby
   - If you die: you kept the armor; just try again
4. Talk to Preston on the roof → accept **When Freedom Calls**.

## Part 5 — Settlement 101 (the 15-minute version)

Back in Sanctuary, Preston makes you General of the Minutemen and teaches
settlements. Beginners overthink this. Here's the **minimum viable
settlement**:

1. Open build mode (the workshop in the ruined house), then:
   - **6 beds** (Beds → any bed; place indoors or under roofs)
   - **Water pump ×2** (Resources → Water)
   - **Food:** plant 6 mutfruit trees (Resources → Food)
   - **2 machinegun turrets** (Defense → Turrets)
2. The **Defense number must equal or exceed Food + Water total** or you get
   raided. Turrets solve this forever.
3. Assign settlers: target a settler in build mode → press A on a crop to
   assign. 3 farmers cover everything.
4. **Stop here.** Don't decorate, don't build a city. Settlements are a
   system you can ignore for 100 hours; this 15-minute version covers the
   quest requirements and gives you a free restock point (workbenches store
   all your junk).

Take the **Old Guns** quest later (artillery), but it can wait until ~level 10.

## Part 6 — Systems crash course (read once, reference forever)

- **VATS** (left bumper): slows time, shows % hit chance per body part. AP
  drains. Head shots ×2 damage, legs cripple chargers. Your crit meter (bar
  under the HP) — spend it in VATS on hard targets.
- **Fast travel:** from the Pip-Boy map, to any *discovered* location. Costs
  nothing, but you can't travel while overencumbered or in combat. Fast
  travel is your friend — walking everywhere is for screenshots.
- **Companions:** get **Dogmeat** at Red Rocket truck stop (south of
  Sanctuary, marker appears). Companions carry **150+ lbs** of your loot
  (trade with them), never die (they go down and get back up), and comment on
  your choices. Dogmeat + Sneak = free backstab bonuses.
- **Junk → materials:** at any workshop, "Store all junk" converts junk to
  crafting components automatically. Adhesive, screws, oil, circuitry, duct
  tape = the crafting economy. **Tag for search** what you need.
- **Weapon crafting:** any weapons workbench → attach mods. Mods need
  materials + the **Gun Nut** perk (INT 3). A modded pipe rifle is genuinely
  good early.
- **Power Armor:** fusion cores drain while sprinting in armor. Cores are
  everywhere later (military checkpoints, sold by vendors). When a core hits
  0 you don't lose the armor — you just exit and carry it (it's heavy).
- **Caps:** earn from quests, selling looted guns/ammo at vendors. Vendors
  restock caps every 24–48 in-game hours. Don't buy junk — buy **ammo,
  stimpaks, and fusion cores**.
- **Encumbered?** Drop heavy weapons, give loot to Dogmeat, or fast travel
  (you can fast travel TO a workbench, dump, and continue).

## Part 7 — Critical path walkthrough (spoiler-light)

Objective-level guide with level gates. Do side content whenever a gate says
"level up first."

### Phase A — Sanctuary to Diamond City (Levels 1–6)

1. **When Freedom Calls** → Preston's group at Sanctuary (done in Part 5).
2. Level to ~4–5 with two tiny side quests:
   - **Tenpines Bluff** (Minutemen radiant quest): kill mole rats at
     Corvega's exterior — don't enter the plant yet
   - **Abernathy Farm** (south): retrieve a locket — 2-minute errand
3. **Jewel of the Commonwealth:** head to Diamond City (follow the main
   marker south along the road). On the way you'll pass **Lexington** — a
   super-mutant nest (Corvega plant). At level 5 with the laser musket +
   Power Armor you *can* clear it; otherwise skirt around it on the overpass.
4. Diamond City gate: let the story handle entry (talk to the woman being
   interviewed at the gate).
5. Inside: explore, visit the Dugout Inn, sell junk to the general trader,
   **listen to the radio**, and find the detective agency marker.

### Phase B — The detective (Levels 6–10)

6. **Unlikely Valentine:** follow the marker into the sewers under the city.
   Stealth + shotgun works; take the **Fusion Pulse Charge** reward seriously.
7. **Getting a Clue:** meet **Nick Valentine**, your companion for this arc.
   He's tanky, carries your loot, and has great commentary.
8. **Reunions:** a long investigation chain (Fort Hagen). This is the first
   hard fight — **bring Power Armor + a charged laser musket or a modded
   rifle, level 10+, and quicksave often.** The interior boss hits very hard;
   VATS the legs, then the head.

### Phase C — The middle act (Levels 10–20)

9. **Dangerous Minds → The Glowing Sea:** you *must* have Power Armor (it
   blocks radiation) and ~4 spare fusion cores. Rad-X and RadAway from any
   doctor. Follow the markers; there is no fighting required out there, only
   walking. Stock stimpaks.
10. **Hunter/Hunted → The Molecular Level:** the story hands you a fast
    travel beacon and opens the endgame faction choice. **This is the
    spoiler gate — go to Part 8.**

Along the way, these side quests are the best value for a beginner:

- **The Silver Shroud** (Diamond City, level 10+): free unique weapon + the
  funniest quest in the game
- **The Secret of Cabot House** (Cabot House, northeast of Diamond City,
  level 10+): strong unique rewards; say yes to the strange serum
- **Old Guns** (Sanctuary, level 10+): artillery for your settlement — call
  it in anywhere, it's absurd and delightful
- **Out in Left Field / radiant Minutemen quests:** skip most; do them only
  if you want settlement XP

## Part 8 — ⚠️ MAJOR SPOILERS: factions & endgame ⚠️

*Stop reading here if you want the reveal fresh. You were warned.*

The Institute — the boogeyman of the Commonwealth — took your son because
their leader **is** your son, aged 60+ years after being raised by them. The
endgame is a faction war between:

| Faction | Style | Beginner verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Minutemen** | Citizen militia, settlements, artillery | **Recommended.** Most forgiving endgame, keeps all others alive-ish, fits "help people" play |
| **Brotherhood of Steel** | Airship, power armor, big guns | Great if you love Power Armor and spectacle; quests are combat-heavy |
| **Railroad** | Synth liberation, stealth | Shortest, stealth-flavored, morally tidy |
| **Institute** | The villains-ish; tech utopia | For second playthroughs |

**Beginner path:** do the Railroad's *Road to Freedom* (follow the Freedom
Trail from Boston Common — the trail markers on the ground spell the code)
and the Brotherhood's *Fire Support/Call to Arms* to meet everyone, then
commit to the **Minutemen** when the story forces the choice. Their ending
requires the least combat min-maxing and leaves the world most intact.

Endgame tips: stock fusion cores, ammo, and stimpaks; the finale is a big
set-piece fight. Quicksave before committing to any faction's final quest —
the point of no return is clearly flagged but final.

## Part 10 — UX habits, troubleshooting

- **Quicksave (L5) constantly.** FO4 is stable but not immortal.
- **Stutter in downtown Boston:** normal. The 40 fps cap absorbs most of it.
  If it bothers you, fast travel to a district edge and walk in.
- **Crash to desktop?** Relaunch; you lose ≤2 minutes with quicksaving. If
  crashes repeat: verify game files (Steam → Properties → Installed Files),
  confirm Weapon Debris is Off.
- **Overencumbered mid-dungeon:** drop the heaviest 3 weapons, grab them on
  your way out (they don't despawn).
- **Lost?** Pip-Boy map → zoom → your active quest marker is the chevron.
  The main quest can always be restarted from the marker; the Commonwealth
  rewards wandering.
- **Session length:** FO4 is perfect in 40–60 min couch sessions — one
  dungeon or one settlement pass per session. Quicksave before putting the
  Deck to sleep anyway.

Welcome to the Commonwealth, Commander. War never changes — but your aim
will, once the gyro kicks in.
